01 / THE IDEA

Less constraint.
More discovery.

Continuous hidden states.
More exploratory visual reasoning.

What if visual reasoning could explore beyond a predefined path?

Latent visual reasoning lets multimodal models think through continuous hidden states instead of decoding every intermediate step into text. Yet hard alignment to predefined visual features can compress these states into a homogeneous space, limiting exploration.

CoLVR—Contrastive Optimization for Latent Visual Reasoning—uses relative contrastive relationships to guide reasoning while preserving flexibility. Angle-based negatives shape the latent space during supervised fine-tuning, and a trajectory contrastive reward carries that principle into reinforcement learning.

Read the full abstract +

Due to the potential for exploratory reasoning of Latent Visual Reasoning, recent works tend to enable MLLMs (Multimodal Large Language Models) to perform visual reasoning by propagating continuous hidden states instead of decoding intermediate steps into discrete tokens. However, existing works typically rely on hard alignment objectives to force latent representations to match predefined visual features, thereby severely limiting the exploratory of latent reasoning process. To address this problem, we propose CoLVR (Contrastive Optimization for Latent Visual Reasoning). To obtain a more exploratory visual reasoning, CoLVR introduces a latent contrastive training framework. Firstly, CoLVR learns diverse and exploratory representations with a latent contrastive objective guided by angle-based perturbation, which expands the semantic latent space and avoids over-constrained embedding. Then, CoLVR employs a latent trajectory contrastive reward for RL (Reinforcement Learning) post-training to enable fine-grained optimization of latent visual reasoning process and thus fostering diverse reasoning behaviors. Experiments demonstrate that CoLVR significantly enhances the exploratory capability of latent representations, achieving average improvements of 5.83% on VSP and 8.00% on Jigsaw, while also outperforming existing latent models on out of domain benchmarks, with a 3.40% gain on MMStar. The data, codes, and models are released at github.com/Oscar-dzy/CoLVR.

05 / THE VISUAL TASKS

Plan. Complete.
Imagine.

Three ways to put visual reasoning to the test.

01Visual Spatial PlanningFind a path through a visual maze+

Navigate grid environments from start to goal. Levels 3–6 evaluate seen settings; harder levels 7–8 test generalization. Result-hint images provide guidance during training.

02JigsawRecover missing visual information+

Identify the correct pieces for missing regions of an image. Solving the puzzle requires global visual context and the ability to imagine a completed scene.

03TetrisReason about constrained spatial completion+

Select pieces that fill the target shape without gaps or overlap. This task tests spatial composition and structural reasoning. The source tables label this benchmark “Tertis”.
